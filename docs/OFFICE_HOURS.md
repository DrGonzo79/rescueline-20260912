# Office Hours: RescueLine

Generated 2026-09-12 · Startup mode · evidence-limited draft

## Position

This is only compelling as a **revenue-recovery queue**, not another call-analytics dashboard. Owners do not need more transcripts; they need the next valuable call placed before the lead hires someone else.

## Six forcing questions

1. **Demand reality:** The source reports AI-receptionist adoption growth and high abandonment after unresolved calls, but no primary customer evidence is available. The current evidence supports a concierge test, not a software company yet.
2. **Painful status quo:** A plumbing/HVAC dispatcher opens the phone vendor dashboard, skims transcripts, guesses which hangups matter, copies numbers into a spreadsheet or CRM, and manually assigns callbacks. Misses are invisible and attribution is weak.
3. **Desperate specificity:** The first customer is the service manager at a 5–30 technician plumbing or HVAC company using Vapi/Retell (or an agency-built equivalent). They are judged on booked jobs, response time, and technician utilization. An emergency lead lost overnight can be worth hundreds or thousands of dollars.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** Every weekday by 8 a.m., deliver a ranked list of unresolved calls, failure reason, estimated job value, and one recommended human action. Charge only after a 14-day concierge replay proves recovered bookings.
5. **Observation plan:** Sit beside three dispatchers for a full morning. Do not demo. Watch how they inspect the prior day’s calls, which calls they skip, how they decide urgency, and whether a suggested action changes behavior. Log time-to-first-callback and booked revenue.
6. **Future-fit:** As voice agents handle more front-desk volume, exception handling becomes more—not less—important. The durable product is the recovery feedback loop that teaches routing and operating policy, not a generic transcript classifier.

## Premise challenge

- The real problem is not “AI sometimes fails.” It is “no accountable human recovery loop exists after an automated interaction fails.”
- Doing nothing leaks high-intent demand and hides whether the receptionist is helping or hurting conversion.
- The wedge fails if existing phone vendors make missed-opportunity ranking reliable and workflow-native; differentiation must come from cross-vendor outcome data and vertical operating rules.

## Alternatives considered

### A. Daily rescue digest — minimal viable
Parse post-call records and send a ranked morning list. **Effort:** S · **Risk:** Low. Fastest proof, but weak workflow ownership and limited learning.

### B. Dispatcher recovery desk — recommended
Interactive queue with rationale, recommended action, ownership, and outcome capture. **Effort:** M · **Risk:** Medium. Owns the closed loop while staying narrow.

### C. Autonomous recovery agent — ideal architecture
Place calls/texts, generate quotes, book jobs, and retrain routing automatically. **Effort:** XL · **Risk:** High. Powerful, but consent, brand, safety, integration, and attribution risks are premature.

## Assignment

Replay 100 real, consented post-call records from one operator. Have a dispatcher label handled/follow-up/lost before seeing the system’s ranking, then measure precision on the top 10 and booked revenue recovered within 24 hours.
